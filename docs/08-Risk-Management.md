# Risk Management

## Overview

Risk Management is the systematic process of identifying, assessing, prioritizing, and mitigating cybersecurity risks that could impact an organization's information systems, business operations, or reputation. Since no system can be completely secure, organizations focus on reducing risks to an acceptable level through security controls, policies, and continuous monitoring.

Effective risk management enables organizations to make informed decisions, allocate security resources efficiently, comply with regulations, and prepare for potential cyber incidents.

---

# What is Risk?

In cybersecurity, **risk** is the possibility that a threat will exploit a vulnerability and cause harm to an organization's assets.

A risk is generally determined by three components:

- **Asset** – Anything valuable to an organization.
- **Threat** – A potential source of harm.
- **Vulnerability** – A weakness that can be exploited.

```text
          Asset
            │
            ▼
      Vulnerability
            │
            ▼
         Threat
            │
            ▼
          Risk
```

---

# Key Terminology

| Term | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Asset | Valuable resource such as data, hardware, software, or people |
| Threat | Any event or actor capable of causing harm |
| Vulnerability | Weakness that can be exploited |
| Risk | Probability of damage occurring |
| Impact | Severity of the damage caused |
| Mitigation | Actions taken to reduce risk |

# Risk Assessment

Risk assessment is the process of evaluating the likelihood and impact of identified risks.

### Step 1 – Identify Assets

Examples:

- Customer databases
- Employee records
- Servers
- Network infrastructure
- Cloud applications

---

### Step 2 – Identify Threats

Examples:

- Malware
- Phishing
- Insider attacks
- Data breaches
- DDoS attacks

---

### Step 3 – Identify Vulnerabilities

Examples:

- Weak passwords
- Outdated software
- Open network ports
- Misconfigured firewalls
- Lack of employee training

---

### Step 4 – Evaluate Risk

Organizations estimate:

- Likelihood of occurrence
- Business impact
- Overall risk level

---

# Risk Matrix

A Risk Matrix helps prioritize risks based on their likelihood and impact.

| Impact ↓ / Likelihood → | Low | Medium | High |
|--------------------------|-----|--------|------|
| Low | Low | Low | Medium |
| Medium | Low | Medium | High |
| High | Medium | High | Critical |

Organizations focus first on **High** and **Critical** risks.

# Risk Treatment Strategies

There are four primary ways to manage cybersecurity risks.

## 1. Risk Avoidance

Avoid activities that create unnecessary risk.

**Example:**

Disabling an insecure legacy application.

---

## 2. Risk Mitigation

Implement security controls to reduce the likelihood or impact of risks.

Examples:

- Firewalls
- Antivirus software
- Multi-Factor Authentication
- Encryption

---

## 3. Risk Transfer

Transfer financial responsibility to another party.

Examples:

- Cyber insurance
- Outsourcing security services

---

## 4. Risk Acceptance

Accept the risk when mitigation costs outweigh potential losses.

Organizations document accepted risks and monitor them regularly.

# Real-World Example

## Equifax Data Breach (2017)

Equifax suffered one of the largest data breaches in history after attackers exploited an unpatched web application vulnerability.

### Impact

- Personal information of approximately 147 million individuals exposed
- Financial losses exceeding hundreds of millions of dollars
- Significant reputational damage
- Increased regulatory scrutiny

### Lessons Learned

- Apply software patches promptly
- Conduct regular vulnerability assessments
- Continuously monitor systems
- Strengthen incident response capabilities

## Summary

Risk Management is a fundamental aspect of cybersecurity that enables organizations to identify, evaluate, and reduce potential threats before they result in significant damage. By combining structured risk assessments, security controls, vulnerability management, and continuous monitoring, organizations can strengthen their security posture, protect critical assets, and maintain long-term operational resilience against evolving cyber threats.
